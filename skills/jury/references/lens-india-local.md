# Lens: India, local and small business

Used when the case file says the region is India, or the business is local or
physical (food, retail, tuition, services, small manufacturing, agriculture),
or a side hustle run alongside a day job.

The judge appends the matching section below to that juror's prompt, under the
heading `LENS`. Jurors apply it on top of their normal job.

General instruction for every juror (included in all sections): work in rupees
(₹). Use lakh and crore for large sums. Do not assume any government rule, fee,
threshold or tax rate from memory: look it up on the official site, cite it as
sourced [S], or mark it unknown [U] and tell the founder to verify it. Rules
change and differ by state.

## advocate
Look for the neighbourhood-level wedge: one apartment complex cluster, one
industrial estate, one college belt, one market street. In smaller cities,
personal trust and word of mouth can beat paid marketing. What can this founder
do that a large aggregator cannot: be reachable on WhatsApp, speak the local
language, extend credit to regulars, show up in person?

## prosecutor
Check the local failure patterns: customers who compare prices with the
neighbourhood vendor, incumbents who already give free delivery or credit,
aggregators that can undercut locally, seasonality (festival months and slack
months), perishable wastage, staff who leave after training, rent that rises
once the location works. Ask whether demand is real in this town or only in
the metros the founder reads about.

## customer
Speak as a buyer in this town and income bracket. Where do I buy this today?
Who do I trust? Do I pay by UPI, cash or monthly credit? Would I pay in
advance, or only after I try it? What price feels fair compared with what my
neighbours pay? Which language do I want to be addressed in? Do I order by
phone call, WhatsApp or app?

## market-analyst
Count competitors locally, not globally. Search map listings and local
directories for the town and the category, and check whether national
aggregators or chains already serve this area (for food and services, look at
the large delivery and home-services apps). Size the market bottom-up from
households, offices or shops within a realistic radius. State the town tier
(metro, tier-2, tier-3) and say how that changes price and volume. Use ₹ price
benchmarks from real listings and cite them.

## growth-strategist
Local channels: WhatsApp groups, apartment and residents' communities,
Instagram and Google Business Profile, local newspapers and hoardings, tie-ups
with offices, schools, temples and trade associations, referrals from existing
customers, free tastings or demos. Estimate cost per customer in ₹, including
the founder's unpaid hours. Name two festivals or seasons that change demand.

## cfo
Work in ₹ per month. Include: rent and deposit, utilities, equipment, packaging,
raw material with wastage, delivery cost, staff pay, payment-gateway and bank
charges, licences and registrations, and the founder's own labour valued
honestly. Working capital matters: how many days between spending on stock
and receiving cash? Check current UPI merchant charge rules and cite them
rather than assuming. Compute a break-even in units per day and a payback
period on the upfront spend. State whether the founder keeps their day job
while testing.

## builder
Can this run with the founder plus one or two people? Name supplier
reliability, power and water, space, staff availability and skills, and what
happens when the founder is sick. Prefer a pre-order or home-scale version
before renting a shop or buying equipment. What is the smallest daily batch
that tests demand without losing money?

## risk-officer
List the registrations and licences that may apply, and verify each on the
official site, citing it: food business licensing (FSSAI, fssai.gov.in), GST
registration and thresholds (gst.gov.in), MSME or Udyam registration
(udyamregistration.gov.in), state shops-and-establishments registration, local
body trade licence (corporation or municipality), fire and pollution
clearances for some premises, and labour rules if hiring. Check home-based
business rules for the category. Flag the point at which an informal start
becomes a compliance problem. Say where a chartered accountant or lawyer is
needed. Only call something a deal-breaker if it genuinely blocks operation.
