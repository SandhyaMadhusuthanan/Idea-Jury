# Worked example: a home-style tiffin service for office workers, Coimbatore

> **Read this first.** This is a hand-worked example in the report format, not
> the output of a real jury run and not market research. Every rupee figure is
> an invented placeholder, tagged [A], so you can see how the method works.
> Replace the numbers with your own. Local facts about Coimbatore (how many
> offices are within reach, which tiffin services already exist, what they
> charge) are marked [U]: the first job is to find out.

## The idea
A home cook prepares 40-45 lunch boxes a day and delivers them to office
workers within a few kilometres, on a weekly prepaid plan. The founder keeps
their day job for the first three months. Goal: ₹30,000 a month extra.

## Unit economics, per meal [A]
| item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Price | 100 |
| Food and packaging | 55 |
| Delivery | 10 |
| **Left over per meal** | **35** |

Fixed costs [A]: ₹8,000 a month (part-time help, gas, packaging contingency).
Working days: 26 a month.

## The arithmetic
- Break-even: 8,000 ÷ 35 = **about 229 meals a month, roughly 9 a day**
- To earn the ₹30,000 goal: (30,000 + 8,000) ÷ 35 = **about 1,086 meals a month, roughly 42 a day**
- At about 22 meals per subscriber per month: **about 50 regular subscribers**
- Cooking and delivery take about 3.5 hours a day [A], so about 91 hours a month
- If the goal is reached: 30,000 ÷ 91 = **about ₹330 per hour** [A]
- Reaching 50 subscribers at a 10% conversion [A] means contacting about 490 people, which is about 20 working days at 25 contacts a day

Read it honestly: at 9 meals a day you are only covering costs. The ₹330 an
hour only exists if you reach about 42 meals a day, so reach is the question,
not cooking.

## What would have to be true
| # | assumption | tag | cheap way to find out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enough office workers near the founder will pay ₹100 for a home-style lunch | U | ask 30 of them for a prepaid week |
| 2 | Food and packaging can be held to about ₹55 per meal at 40 meals a day | A | cook 10 trial meals and weigh the cost |
| 3 | Delivery averages about ₹10 per meal | A | do one real delivery round and time it |
| 4 | Existing tiffin services and canteens are not good enough or cheap enough to stop switching | U | map and price-check them |
| 5 | The founder can cook and deliver 40+ meals a day around a day job | A | do a full-scale day once |
| 6 | Home-based food sale is permitted at this volume without extra licensing | U | check the food licensing rules on the official site and ask a local professional |

All six are assumed or unknown. This is an idea to test, not to start.

## Cheapest test (3 weeks)
Offer a one-week prepaid plan, ₹500 for five lunches, to 30 office workers
the founder can actually reach. Cook only for those who pay. Spend about ₹550
on 10 trial meals to measure real cost.

## Kill criteria, written before starting
| # | metric | stop if | by when | then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | people who prepay for a week | fewer than 5 of 30 | day 21 | stop |
| 2 | measured cost per meal over 10 trial meals | above ₹65 | day 10 | reprice or stop |
| 3 | meals one person can deliver in a lunch window without help | fewer than 20 | day 14 | pivot to pick-up points |

If 5 or more of 30 prepay, the next step is 15 regular subscribers, not a shop.

## Verdict (as the jury would likely word it)
**TEST FIRST.** No buyer has paid, so GO is not available. The idea is not a
KILL because the arithmetic can work. The decisive unknown is whether enough
reachable people will pay in advance. Everything else is cheaper to learn after
that.
